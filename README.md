# Price of Play

A free browser card game about one number: cost per play. You bankroll a game group's shelf for six rounds. Each round you draft a box at its real-style price, then a life card decides how often the table actually plays it. Price divided by plays settles the round against a benchmark line, roughly what a movie ticket costs.

A $249 premium game played four times a year for five years works out near $12 per session. A $20 filler played once costs $20 flat. Price alone tells you almost nothing; frequency and longevity do the real work. That division is the whole game, and it is the same method behind every budget estimate at [poketdev.com](https://poketdev.com).

Sources for the numbers: Petersen Games, "Are Premium Board Games Worth the Money? A Cost-Per-Play Guide" (2026), and Meeple Mountain, "Cost Per Cackle" (2025).

## Play

Open `index.html` in any browser, or load the GitHub Pages build. Three cards per round, click one, read your life card, next round. Six picks, then the shelf settles.

## Rules in one paragraph

Each round deals three purchase cards with prices from $20 to $249. Pick one and draw a life card: plays per year, years in rotation, plus event cards such as an expansion drop, a friend group adopting the game, or the worst card in the deck, played once and shelved, which scores its full price as cost per play. Lowest cost per play under the round's ticket line scores a win. Six rounds later the game totals your spend, your sessions, and how many picks earned their shelf spot.
